Resume Writing Tips

Best practices that make your resume stand out to both humans and ATS systems.

🎯Use a strong professional summary▾
Write 2–3 sentences that immediately communicate who you are and what you offer. Tailor it to every role. Example: "Results-driven software engineer with 5 years building scalable Python APIs. Reduced infra cost by 40% at TechCorp. Seeking senior backend roles in fintech."
  • Put it at the very top, after your contact info
  • Match keywords from the job description
  • Focus on value delivered, not duties
🔢Quantify your achievements▾
Numbers make your impact concrete and memorable. Replace vague descriptions with measurable results.
  • ❌ "Responsible for improving the checkout process"
  • ✅ "Reduced checkout abandonment by 22% by redesigning the payment flow"
  • ❌ "Led a team of engineers"
  • ✅ "Led a team of 6 engineers to deliver a $2M product launch on time"
  • Use metrics wherever you can substantiate them
📐Structure and formatting best practices▾
ATS systems and recruiters both benefit from clear structure.
  • Use clear section headings: Work Experience, Education, Skills, Projects
  • Reverse chronological order (most recent first)
  • Consistent bullet points — avoid mixing styles
  • Single column for ATS compatibility (avoid tables and text boxes)
  • Keep to 1–2 pages for most roles
  • Font: 10–12pt, standard typefaces (not decorative)
  • Save as PDF unless the job posting specifies otherwise
🔑Keywords and ATS optimisation▾
Many companies use ATS software that filters resumes by keyword match before a human sees them.
  • Use ResumeLens keyword analysis to find missing terms
  • Mirror the exact language from the job description where accurate
  • Include both long-form and abbreviated versions: "Machine Learning (ML)"
  • Avoid images, headers/footers with key info, and tables — ATS may skip them
  • Don't keyword-stuff — write naturally; only include terms that reflect your real experience
🛠️Skills section guide▾
A well-structured skills section helps both ATS and hiring managers quickly assess your fit.
  • Group by category: Languages, Frameworks, Tools, Cloud, Soft Skills
  • List skills you'd be comfortable discussing in an interview
  • Don't over-inflate — listing a skill you can't demonstrate hurts you
  • Include tools and technologies from your most recent 3–5 years
⚡
Action Verb Starters
Start every bullet with a strong verb: Built, Designed, Led, Reduced, Shipped, Optimised, Launched, Automated, Improved, Scaled, Mentored, Delivered.
Quick Reference
📏
Ideal Resume Length
0–5 years experience: 1 page. 5–10 years: 1–2 pages. 10+ years or senior/exec roles: up to 2 pages. Academic CVs can be longer.
Best Practice
🔄
Tailor for Every Job
Generic resumes underperform. Spend 10 minutes swapping in role-specific keywords and reordering bullet points to match each application.

Cover Letter Framework

A great cover letter answers one question: why are you the right person for this specific role?

📝 The 4-Paragraph Structure
1
The Hook

State the role and why you want it specifically. Mention something specific about the company — a product, mission, or recent news. Shows you've done your homework.

2
Your Strongest Match

Pick your 1–2 most relevant experiences and explain how they map to the key requirements. Use numbers. This is the most important paragraph.

3
Why This Company

Explain what excites you about this specific team or company. Culture, product, problem space, or growth stage. Avoid generic praise.

4
The Close

Thank them for their time, reiterate your enthusiasm, and request an interview. Keep it short and direct.

❌
Common Mistakes to Avoid
• Starting with "I am writing to apply…"
• Repeating your resume line-by-line
• Writing more than one page
• Forgetting to address it to a specific person
• Spelling the company name wrong
Avoid These
✨
Opening Line Examples
Strong openers that grab attention:

"After building data pipelines that process 10M events/day, I'm excited to bring that scale experience to [Company]'s data platform team."

"Your recent launch of [Product] is exactly the kind of problem I've been solving — here's why I'm the right engineer for the role."
Templates
📏
Length & Format
• 3–4 paragraphs, never more than 1 page
• Use the same font as your resume
• Short paragraphs (3–5 sentences max)
• Address to hiring manager by name if possible (check LinkedIn)
• Send as PDF

Interview Preparation

Practice these questions and frameworks before your next interview.

🗣️ Behavioural Questions (STAR Method)
Answer with: Situation → Task → Action → Result
💻 Technical Interview Tips
🗣️
Think out loud

Interviewers want to see your process. Narrate your reasoning even when you're unsure.

❓
Clarify before coding

Ask about edge cases, input formats, constraints. It shows engineering maturity.

🐢
Start with brute force

Get a working solution first, then optimise. A correct O(n²) beats a broken O(n log n).

🧪
Test your solution

Walk through examples, including edge cases, before declaring done. Shows diligence.

📊
Discuss complexity

Always state the time and space complexity of your solution. Shows CS fundamentals.

How to Negotiate
• Always try to get a number from them first
• Anchor high — your first number sets the range
• Use competing offers or market data as leverage
• Negotiate the full package: equity, bonus, PTO, remote
• Get it in writing before accepting

Total Compensation
At larger companies, base salary can be just 40–60% of total comp. Equity (RSUs/options), annual bonus, signing bonus, and benefits can equal or exceed base salary. Always evaluate the full package.
